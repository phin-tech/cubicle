## Stash

Tuck a window away against the left, right or top edge of its display, and pop it out when you want it, like
Rectangle Pro's stash. A stashed window leaves its workspace and waits behind a small Tab on the edge, which shows on
every workspace.

- **Stash the window in front** with ⌃⌥⌘[ (left), ⌃⌥⌘] (right) or ⌃⌥⌘\ (top). The same shortcut again unstashes it;
  another moves it to that edge.
- **Point at its Tab** and the window slides out beside it; move away and it tucks back. **Click** the Tab or the
  window to keep it out until you click somewhere else.
- **Unstash** with its shortcut, from the Tab's right-click menu, by dragging the popped-out window away from the edge,
  or with `cubicle unstash`. It lands on the workspace showing there.
- **Drag a Tab** anywhere along an edge, onto another edge or display, or onto other Tabs to line up with them.
  The right-click menu also puts it at the top, middle or bottom, or on another edge.
- **Three looks** for Tabs: the app's icon, the icon and the window's title, or a **sticky note** in the app's colour
  (picked from its icon, or chosen from the Tab's menu) with its name written along the edge.
- **Settings > Stash** has the look, your shortcuts, every stashed window (move or unstash it from there) and the note
  colours you've chosen.
- From the command line: `cubicle stash right [--position top] [--app Herdr]`, `cubicle stashed` and
  `cubicle unstash`. Alfred's snap results include Stash Left, Right and Top.

Stashed windows come back stashed when Cubicle restarts, and quitting Cubicle puts them back where they were.
