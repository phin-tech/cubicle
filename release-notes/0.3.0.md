## Workspaces replace Spaces

Cubicle no longer switches macOS Spaces. Each display gets its own workspaces, and switching hides the other
workspaces' windows just past a corner of the screen, the way AeroSpace and Spaceman do. It's instant, with no Space
animation, and moving a window to another workspace happens straight away.

**Before you update:** keep one macOS desktop per display (remove the extras in Mission Control, with ⌃↑), because
Cubicle can't see windows on the others. Workspace names start blank, since they aren't macOS Spaces.

- **Everything that switched Spaces now switches workspaces:** the shortcuts, left and right, the grid, search, the
  menu bar and `cubicle go`. ⌘-Tab or a Dock click on a window on another workspace takes you there.
- **Trackpad swipes** with three or four fingers switch workspaces (Settings > General).
- **As many workspaces as you like.** Each display starts with 8 and keeps its own count. Add one with ⌃N in the
  grid or `cubicle new-space`; remove one with ⌃⌫ twice in the grid or `cubicle remove-space`, and its windows join
  the workspace before it. Only the first 16 have shortcuts.
- **Rename from the grid** with ⌃R.
- **New workspace from windows:** ⌃⌥⌘N, then click the windows you want, press Return and name it.
- **Move a workspace to the other display** with its windows, name and bindings (menu bar, ⌃⌥⌘M, or
  `cubicle move-workspace`).
- **Switches don't flash:** the new workspace's windows come back before the old ones leave.
- **Quitting Cubicle shows every window again**, and a window left hidden by a crash is brought back.

## Window snapping

Rectangle-style snapping: halves, quarters, Maximize, Center and Restore, with Rectangle's keys (⌃⌥ + arrows, U I J K,
↩, C, ⌫), plus **Full Screen** (⌃⌥F). It covers the whole display and hides the menu bar and Dock while the window
stays on its workspace. Press it again to put the window back. The shortcuts are off until you turn them on in
Settings > Keyboard Shortcuts. Snaps are also in the ⌃⌥⌘-click window menu and `cubicle snap`, and **Settings >
General > Snap gap** leaves a gap between windows.

## Stacking

Every zone can hold a stack of windows. **Bind** an app or a window to a zone on a workspace and its windows go there
when they open, even windows that open on another workspace. Cycle a stack with ⌃⌥] and ⌃⌥[, swap zones with ⌃⌥⇧ +
arrows, and pause with ⌃⌥P. `cubicle layout` shows what's bound where.

## Alfred

Re-import the workflow. **`cb`** opens Cubicle's menu: **`cb windows`** lists every window on every workspace; Return
goes to one, and Tab sends it to another workspace or onto a new one you name. It also goes to, sends to, renames,
adds and removes workspaces, and snaps. `@mail` jumps to a workspace and `w left` snaps the window you were in. The
menu comes from the `cubicle` command, so it stays up to date with the app, and the keywords can be changed in
Alfred's Configure Workflow.

## Command line

New: `cubicle focus`, `send --window`, `new-workspace`, `rename`, `remove-space`, `move-workspace`,
`pick-workspace`, `windows` (every workspace, with where each window is), `snap`, `bind`, `cycle`, `swap`, `pause`
and `layout`.
